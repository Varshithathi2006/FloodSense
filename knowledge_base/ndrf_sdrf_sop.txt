# NDRF and SDRF Standard Operating Procedures for Flood Search, Rescue and Evacuation Operations in India
Document ID: NDRF-SDRF-SOP-WATER-RESCUE-2024
Authority: National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), Ministry of Home Affairs; State Disaster Response Force Directorates
Reference: NDRF SOP for Flood Relief Operations 2022; DM Act 2005 (Section 44-45)

[SECTION-1.0] NDRF SEARCH AND RESCUE (SAR) MOBILIZATION MATRIX
1.1 NDRF Water Rescue Team (WRT) Composition:
- Each NDRF Water Rescue Team comprises: 1 Team Commander (Inspector rank), 4 Deep-Diver Certified Rescue Specialists, 2 Medical First Responders (MFR with BLS/ACLS), 1 Dog Squad handler (cadaver/survivor detection), 2 Inflatable Motor Boats with 40 HP Outboard Motors (OBM), 1 Satellite Phone, and 1 UAV (Quadcopter) for aerial reconnaissance.
- NDRF Battalions: 16 battalions deployed across India. Key flood-prone battalion locations: 4th Bn (Arakkonam, Tamil Nadu), 6th Bn (Vadodara, Gujarat), 8th Bn (Guwahati, Assam), 9th Bn (Patna, Bihar), 11th Bn (Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh), 12th Bn (Mundali, Odisha).

1.2 Response Time Targets (NDRF Operational Standard):
- Within 4 hours of Level-2 (Severe) alert: NDRF advance team (10 personnel + boats) at staging area.
- Within 12 hours of Level-3 (Critical) alert: Full NDRF column (45 personnel + vehicles + boats + medical) operational.
- Pre-positioning protocol: NDRF teams pre-positioned at risk-prone districts before onset of monsoon season (June 1) based on IMD long-range monsoon forecast.

1.3 SDRF Activation Thresholds:
- SDRF (State Disaster Response Force) activated on State Government order under DM Act 2005 Section 44.
- SDRF first-responders reach site within 2 hours for district-level events; NDRF supplemented when SDRF capacity exceeded.
- SDRF-equipped districts: Each flood-prone state maintains SDRF battalions (e.g., Kerala SDRF: Thiruvananthapuram; Assam SDRF: Guwahati; Bihar SDRF: Patna; Odisha SDRF: Bhubaneswar).

[SECTION-2.0] STAGING OPERATIONS AND FORWARD BASE ESTABLISHMENT
2.1 Base Camp Alpha (Rear Logistics):
- Established at nearest non-flooded, paved intersection with multi-axle vehicle accessibility.
- Coordinates with District EOC (DEOC), State EOC (SEOC), and NDMA NEOC.
- Equipped with: Satellite communication gear, ration stores, medical supplies, generator sets, fuel storage.

2.2 Forward Wet Base (Bravo):
- Established at flood water's edge boundary for launching inflatable rescue crafts.
- Minimum safety equipment: Personal flotation devices (PFD/life jacket for all personnel), rope-throw bags, swimmer rescue tethers.

[SECTION-3.0] VEHICULAR SUBMERSION AND OCCUPANT EXTRACTION PROTOCOL
3.1 Submerged Vehicle Protocol (India):
- Any vehicle in floodwaters where water exceeds hubcap or door sill (~20–30 cm) is HIGH RISK for occupant trapping due to hydraulic door seal pressure.
- Rescuers approach against water current using safety tether lines tied to fixed anchor point.
- Immediate glass-punch extraction required if electronic door locks have short-circuited (standard in Indian monsoon flash floods).
- Vehicles submerged above window level: Mandatory diver survey to confirm zero human occupants before salvage marking.
- Special context (India): Two-wheelers (motorcycles, scooters) are the most commonly submerged vehicles in Indian urban flooding; occupant is typically already clear — prioritize vehicle removal to prevent road blockage.

[SECTION-4.0] PRIORITY TRIAGE FOR STRANDED CIVILIANS IN INDIA
4.1 Triage Hierarchy (NDRF Standard):
- Priority 1 RED (Immediate): Individuals stranded on single-story rooftops with rising water, persons requiring urgent medical support (dialysis patients, oxygen-dependent patients, trauma victims), pregnant women, unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities.
- Priority 2 YELLOW (Urgent): Stranded residents on multi-story building balconies without immediate rising water threat, elderly persons above age 60, individuals without food/water for more than 24 hours.
- Priority 3 GREEN (Non-Urgent): Individuals in secure multi-story buildings with stable food and potable water supply, awaiting water recession.

4.2 India-Specific Vulnerable Groups:
- Residents of slum settlements (jhuggi-jhopdi clusters) and low-income urban housing near nullahs (drains) and riverbanks: highest-priority evacuation.
- Agricultural labourers and families in riverine floodplains (khadar land areas) of Ganga-Yamuna doab, Brahmaputra plains, Godavari delta.
- Coastal fishing communities in cyclone-flood combination events (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal coasts).

[SECTION-5.0] DEBRIS AND OBSTRUCTION CLEARANCE PROTOCOL
5.1 Waterway Clearing:
- Submerged tree limbs, floating waste (including cattle carcasses in rural flood scenarios), and collapsed temporary structures (jhuggi walls, bamboo fencing) obstructing rescue boat pathways must be cleared with winch-equipped rescue craft.
- Storm drain (nullah) unblocking: BDA/BBMP/MCGM/Chennai Corporation drain-clearing machinery to be deployed within 6 hours to restore gravity drainage.

5.2 Power Line Hazard Protocol (India):
- Rescuers must maintain minimum 10-metre clearance from submerged transformers, overhead sagging 11kV lines, and LT service mains until official clearance from State DISCOM (e.g., KSEB Kerala, TANGEDCO Tamil Nadu, CESC West Bengal, MSEDCL Maharashtra).
- Electrocution risk is significantly elevated in Indian urban flooding due to illegal electrical connections and degraded insulation on overhead LT lines in unplanned settlements.

[SECTION-6.0] INTER-STATE COORDINATION AND DEPLOYMENT
6.1 Interstate NDRF Deployment:
- Centre may deploy NDRF to any State without State request if situation warrants (DM Act 2005 Section 45).
- State SEOC requests NDRF via MHA Disaster Management Division; deployment within 4 hours by road/air.

6.2 Indian Armed Forces Requisition:
- Requisition via State Government to MHA/Ministry of Defence for Indian Army Column deployment.
- Indian Army Rapid Action Medical Teams (RAMT) deploy Mobile Surgical Teams (MST) and Field Hospitals within 24 hours.
- IAF helicopter taskings coordinated via HQ Western/Eastern/Southern Air Command for flood-affected states.
